HHT: a travel survey of one

HHT is a free, open-source iOS app that records where you go, rebuilds each day into visits, trips and travel modes, and keeps all of it on your phone. Think of it as a household travel survey that fills itself in, with a sample size of one. The longer-term aim: travel surveys within reach of anyone who needs one.

What it does today

collect

Background recording

GPS runs only while you are moving. When you stop, it switches to a geofence to save battery.

reconstruct

Visits, trips and modes

A deterministic engine turns raw fixes into stays, trips, mode segments and named places.

correct

One-tap fixes

Change a mode, rename a place, split or merge. Corrections are locked and survive rebuilds.

analyse

Stats and export

Mode share, radius of gyration, tours, flights. Export everything for pandas, R, DuckDB or QGIS.

All of this sits behind a playful "sticker" interface: thick ink outlines, solid shadows, bright flat colours and a friendly map-pin mascot that sums up your day.

Why it matters

Location history is one of the most revealing datasets a person produces. Most apps that collect it are run by companies or individual developers who either charge a subscription, upload your movements to their servers, or both. Once the data is on someone else's server, you are trusting their business model, their security, and whatever they decide to do with it later.

Researchers face a similar problem from the other side. A household travel survey asks people to keep a one- or two-day diary of every trip: where they started, where they went, when, by what mode, and why. Those diaries are expensive, short, and depend on memory, and smartphone survey platforms are usually commercial. HHT keeps the same diary continuously, from GPS and motion sensors, lets you correct it in a tap or two, and publishes every line of code that does it.

Free and open sourceApache 2.0. No subscription, no paid tier, no ads. Read every line that touches your data.
Nothing goes to a cloudNo account, no server, no analytics SDK, no sync. The database is one SQLite file in the app's sandbox. The few network requests are listed on the Privacy page.
You stay in controlYou decide what to correct, what to export, who gets it, and when to erase.
Survey-shaped outputTrips follow household-travel-survey (HHTS) conventions and export to CSV, JSON, GeoJSON and SQLite. Accuracy has been measured, but only on a small sample so far.

Who it serves

  • Anyone who wants a private travel diary without handing their location history to a company.
  • Transportation and urban researchers who want clean, HHTS-style data about their own travel, a small pilot, or a reference implementation of trip reconstruction. Read Research uses & limitations first: HHT data is not a representative survey.
  • Teachers and students learning how trip diaries are built, using the code and synthetic demo data.
  • iOS and Swift developers curious about long-running background location, or wanting to fork the app and extend it.

What is planned

Everything above works today. The ideas below don't exist yet, and the roadmap labels each one as in progress, planned or exploratory.

  • In progress An App Store release, so installing no longer needs Xcode (target: around November 2026).
  • Planned Choosing exactly which dates, trips and level of detail to export.
  • Planned Prompted recall and study configuration for small research projects.
  • Planned A formal accuracy benchmark and reproducible test fixtures.
  • Exploratory A separate research edition beside today's personal app, sharing the same engine.
  • Exploratory Android, multilingual and self-hosted versions, aimed partly at cities and countries with limited budgets or technical staff.

The reasoning behind all of this is on the Vision page.

i

HHT is not on the App Store yet. You build it from source with Xcode and install it on your own phone. With a free Apple ID the install lasts 7 days and you renew it with one command, and your data is kept between installs. See Installation.
