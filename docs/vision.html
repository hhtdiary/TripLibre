Vision

From a travel survey of one to travel surveys within reach

HHT started as one transportation researcher's private travel diary. The longer-term aim is a free, open toolkit that lets anyone collect basic, well-documented travel data without a vendor contract, a server, or giving up control of their location history.

Where HHT is today

HHT is an iOS app that records where its user goes, rebuilds each day into visits, trips and travel modes, lets the user correct it in a tap or two, and exports the result. Everything is stored on the phone. It has one user per install, no account and no server. See what it does for the full list.

That is a personal tool. It is not yet a survey platform: there is no way to enrol participants, configure a study, or collect data from more than one phone. Those ideas are on the roadmap as Planned or Exploratory.

The gap HHT tries to bridge

Three groups need travel data and rarely meet in the same tool:

planners

Transportation planners

Know what a good household travel survey (HHTS) looks like: person-days, trips, modes, purposes, tours. Usually buy the collection from a vendor.

developers

Software developers

Can build reliable background location and clean exports, but rarely see survey conventions or what researchers need to defend a dataset.

researchers

Resource-constrained researchers

Students, small agencies, community groups and teachers who want travel data but can't fund a commercial smartphone-survey platform.

HHT is an attempt to put the planner's data model, the developer's engineering and the researcher's need for transparency into one open codebase.

Local-firstOne SQLite file on the phone. No account, no sync, no analytics SDK. Available
Privacy-firstNetwork access is limited and listed on the Privacy page. Off-device sharing happens only when the user exports. Available
TransparentInference rules are deterministic, documented and tested. Raw data is never rewritten. Available
User-controlledThe user decides what to correct, what to export and when to erase. Finer-grained export is Planned.
AdaptableThresholds live in one config struct and a calibration harness measures changes. Study-specific configuration is Planned.

What HHT is not

Being clear about limits matters more than the vision itself.

  • Not a representative household travel survey. A regional HHTS relies on a sampling frame, recruitment, weighting, household rosters and nonresponse follow-up. HHT provides none of that. It gives you accessible basic data collection: a clean, correctable trip diary from whoever chooses to run it. A set of HHT diaries is a convenience sample unless a study design makes it something more. See Research uses & limitations.
  • Not risk-free. Keeping data on the device removes the server, not the risk. A trace of where someone sleeps and works can re-identify them, the phone can be lost or backed up to iCloud, and an export can be forwarded. HHT reduces privacy exposure; it does not eliminate it.
  • Not validated at research grade. HHT's output follows survey conventions, and its accuracy has been measured on a small personal sample. It has not been benchmarked against a formal ground truth across many people. See Validation.
  • Not a behaviour-change app. No streaks, goals or nudges. It records and summarises.

How we'll get there

Each step stays inside the same principles: data on the device by default, sharing only by the user's explicit choice, and documentation that says what is and isn't true yet. The phases, and the status of each item, are on the Roadmap.
